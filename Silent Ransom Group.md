# Silent Ransom Group
|Source|Date|Details|
| ------ | ------ | ------ |
[Luna Moth Ransomware: The Threat Actors Behind Recent False Subscription Scams](https://www.sygnia.co/blog/luna-moth-false-subscription-scams/) | 01 Jul 2022 | Sygnia publicly identified Luna Moth, also tracked as TG2729 and identifying itself as the Silent Ransom Group (SRG). Researchers assessed that the group had been operating since the end of March 2022 and focused on data theft and extortion rather than traditional file-encrypting ransomware. Initial attacks used phishing emails themed around Zoho, MasterClass and Duolingo subscription renewals, followed by telephone social engineering and legitimate remote-access tools. |
[New ‘Luna Moth’ hackers breach orgs via fake subscription renewals](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/new-luna-moth-hackers-breach-orgs-via-fake-subscription-renewals/) | 12 Jul 2022 | Further reporting on Luna Moth's emerging data-extortion operation. Victims received fake subscription-renewal emails containing invoices and telephone numbers. After calling, victims were socially engineered into installing legitimate remote-access software. Researchers observed Atera, AnyDesk, Syncro and Splashtop alongside SoftPerfect Network Scanner, SharpShares and Rclone for reconnaissance and data theft. |
[Threat Assessment: Luna Moth Callback Phishing Campaign](https://unit42.paloaltonetworks.com/luna-moth-callback-phishing/) | 21 Nov 2022 | Unit 42 documented a campaign operating from mid-May through late October 2022. Luna Moth used callback phishing without deploying ransomware, instead abusing legitimate remote-management tools to gain hands-on access and manually exfiltrate data. Victims included legal and retail organizations, with ransom demands ranging from hundreds of thousands of dollars to 78 BTC. The campaign used VoIP infrastructure, victim-specific phone numbers, Zoho Assist, Syncro, Rclone and WinSCP. |
[Crimeware Report: Trends and Highlights from Q3 2023](https://areteir.com/resources/trends-and-highlights-from-q3-2023) | 01 Nov 2023 | Arete reported that Luna Moth had returned in high volumes during Q3 2023. The group continued using callback phishing, including Peloton-themed lures, and primarily targeted law firms in exfiltration-only extortion attacks. |
[Ransomware Actors Continue to Gain Access through Third Parties and Legitimate System Tools](https://www.ic3.gov/CSA/2023/231108.pdf) | 07 Nov 2023 | The FBI publicly documented Silent Ransom Group, also known as Luna Moth, conducting callback-phishing data-theft and extortion attacks. Victims were sent phishing messages concerning fictitious pending charges and instructed to call an attacker-controlled number. Attackers then directed victims toward legitimate system-management tools, accessed local and network-shared files, exfiltrated data and extorted the victim organization. |
[Flash Report: Threat Collective Conducting In-Person Data Theft](https://www.zerofox.com/intelligence/flash-report-threat-collective-conducting-in-person-data-theft/) | Dec 2024 | ZeroFox identified a leak site called LeakedData and assessed that it was almost certainly associated with Silent Ransom Group. The site used clearnet infrastructure and victim listings that substantially overlapped with organizations later identified as SRG targets. |
[Luna Moth extortion hackers pose as IT help desks to breach US firms](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/luna-moth-extortion-hackers-pose-as-it-help-desks-to-breach-us-firms/) | 05 May 2025 | EclecticIQ research documented an intensified Luna Moth campaign against U.S. legal and financial organizations. The group impersonated IT support through emails, fake websites and telephone calls and relied on legitimate RMM software rather than ransomware. Researchers identified at least 37 domains used to impersonate helpdesk or support portals, with ransom demands reportedly ranging from $1 million to $8 million. |
[Dark Web Profile: Silent Ransom Group (LeakedData)](https://socradar.io/blog/dark-web-profile-silent-ransom-group-leakeddata/) | 15 May 2025 | SOCRadar assessed that LeakedData was likely a rebranded incarnation of Silent Ransom Group. The operation emerged publicly in December 2024 and used a dedicated leak site to list victims, publish stolen information and pressure organizations into paying extortion demands. |
[Silent Ransom Group Targeting Law Firms](https://www.fbi.gov/file-repository/cyber-alerts/silent-ransom-group-targeting-law-firms-052325.pdf/view) | 23 May 2025 | The FBI issued a Private Industry Notification identifying Silent Ransom Group as also known as Luna Moth, Chatty Spider and UNC3753. The FBI stated that SRG had consistently targeted U.S. law firms since spring 2023 and had also victimized medical and insurance organizations. The advisory described IT-support impersonation, social engineering, legitimate remote-access tools, data exfiltration and extortion. |
[Targeted social engineering is en vogue as ransom payment sizes increase](https://coveware.com/2025/07/targeted-social-engineering-is-en-vogue-as-ransom-payment-sizes-increase/) | 01 Jul 2025 | Coveware described Silent Ransom/Luna Moth as a data-theft-only extortion group whose initial-access model relies heavily on social engineering. The group impersonates IT support and persuades employees to install remote-assistance software such as Zoho Assist, Quick Assist or AnyDesk before stealing data and threatening disclosure. |
[Ongoing Targeted Campaign Against US Law Firms](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/threat-intelligence/targeted-campaign-us-law-firms) | 05 Jun 2026 | Mandiant and Google Threat Intelligence Group documented a campaign by UNC3753, also tracked as Luna Moth, Chatty Spider and Silent Ransom Group, operating from January through May 2026 against dozens of U.S. professional, legal and financial organizations. Attackers used vishing, invoice and data-migration pretexts, screen sharing and legitimate RMM utilities. Researchers also observed attempts to obtain physical access to victim offices. |
[FBI Flash Report TLP Clear: Silent Ransom Group Impersonating IT Personnel through Social Engineering](https://www.ic3.gov/CSA/2026/260526.pdf) | 26 May 2026 | The FBI reported a major escalation in SRG tactics. Attackers impersonated victim IT personnel through calls and phishing emails and directed employees to grant remote access. When remote access failed, SRG reportedly sent individuals to victim offices to obtain physical access and insert external storage devices into computers. WinSCP, Rclone, Google Drive and Microsoft OneDrive were identified in the group's data-exfiltration activity. |
[FBI warns extortion hackers are visiting US law firms to steal data](https://therecord.media/fbi-warns-hackers-visit-law-firms-to-steal-data) | 27 May 2026 | Reporting on the FBI's physical-intrusion warning. SRG was reported to be sending individuals to law firms while posing as IT support personnel. These individuals could use USB drives or external hard drives to steal sensitive information when remote-access attempts failed. SRG also pressured victims by contacting employees or clients and threatening publication or sale of stolen information. |
[Flash Report: Threat Collective Conducting In-Person Data Theft](https://www.zerofox.com/intelligence/flash-report-threat-collective-conducting-in-person-data-theft/) | 01 Jun 2026 | ZeroFox analyzed SRG's physical-intrusion campaign and leak infrastructure. Researchers reported that approximately 90% of victims listed between January and May 2026 were from the legal and consulting sectors in the United States and Canada. ZeroFox also linked the group's activity to the LeakedData leak site first identified in December 2024. |
[Silent Ransom Group Uses DNS Fast Flux in Attacks](https://www.securityweek.com/silent-ransom-group-uses-dns-fast-flux-in-attacks/) | 08 Jun 2026 | Resecurity research reported that SRG was using a fast-flux network of compromised devices to conceal command-and-control and leak-site infrastructure. Researchers identified fast-flux nodes across multiple countries and ISPs. The activity was associated with SRG's continued targeting of U.S. law firms through vishing, social engineering and legitimate remote-access tools. |
[Ransomware in focus: Luna Moth](https://www.s-rminform.com/latest-thinking/ransomware-in-focus-luna-moth) | 02 Jul 2026 | S-RM profiled Luna Moth/Silent Ransom Group and its continued concentration on U.S. law firms. The report documented social engineering, telephone and Microsoft Teams impersonation of IT personnel, Quick Assist and AnyDesk, WinSCP, cloud storage and direct pressure against employees as part of the group's data-extortion model. |
[Law firms pay ransoms to Luna Moth](https://www.theinsurer.com/cyber-risk/news/law-firms-pay-ransoms-to-luna-moth-2026-09-22/) | 22 Sep 2026 | The Insurer reported that at least three major law firms had made multimillion-dollar payments to Luna Moth during 2026. Reported figures included at least $18 million from WilmerHale, approximately $10 million from Goodwin Procter and between $18 million and $20 million from Weil Gotshal & Manges. The figures were based on sources familiar with the incidents rather than public confirmation from every affected firm. |
[The Silent Ransom Group Leak: How Money Was Moved](https://crystalintelligence.com/investigations/inside-the-silent-ransom-group-leak-how-ransoms-were-laundered/) | 06 Oct 2026 | Crystal Intelligence analyzed an apparent leak containing 5,692 internal Silent Ransom Group messages covering August 2025 through September 2026. The material reportedly included discussions of ransom payments, cryptocurrency wallets and laundering methods. Internal records claimed approximately $207 million in payments from 27 firms between April and September 2026, although the figure could not be independently verified. Blockchain analysis confirmed that real cryptocurrency transactions corresponded with portions of the leaked communications. |
